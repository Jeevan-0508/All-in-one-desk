# Chapter 12: Risk Evaluation

---

## 12.1 What Is Risk Evaluation

Risk evaluation is the process of:
- Comparing assessed risks against defined criteria
- Determining acceptability
- Deciding which risks require action

Risk evaluation answers:
> “Is this risk acceptable, tolerable, or unacceptable?”

Evaluation connects **analysis to decision-making**.

---

## 12.2 Risk Evaluation vs Risk Assessment

| Aspect | Risk Assessment | Risk Evaluation |
|-----|-----------------|----------------|
| Purpose | Measure risk | Judge risk |
| Output | Scores, insights | Decisions |
| Question | How severe is it? | What should we do? |

Assessment informs evaluation.
Evaluation enables action.

---

## 12.3 Risk Criteria

Risk criteria define:
- Acceptable risk levels
- Tolerance thresholds
- Escalation triggers

Criteria may be based on:
- Financial limits
- Regulatory requirements
- Safety thresholds
- Reputation sensitivity
- Strategic importance

Without criteria, evaluation is subjective.

---

## 12.4 Risk Appetite and Tolerance in Evaluation

Risk appetite sets:
- The overall boundary of acceptable risk

Risk tolerance sets:
- Operational limits within that boundary

Evaluation compares **residual risk** against appetite and tolerance.

---

## 12.5 Acceptable, Tolerable, and Unacceptable Risk

### Acceptable Risk
- Low impact or likelihood
- Within appetite
- No additional action required

### Tolerable Risk
- Manageable with controls
- Requires monitoring
- Action may be needed

### Unacceptable Risk
- Exceeds appetite
- Requires immediate treatment or escalation

Most risk decisions fall in the **tolerable** zone.

---

## 12.6 Risk Ranking and Prioritization

Evaluation ranks risks to:
- Focus resources
- Address critical exposures first

Ranking considers:
- Severity
- Velocity
- Interdependencies
- Strategic relevance

The highest score is not always the highest priority.

---

## 12.7 Risk Acceptance Decisions

Risk acceptance:
- Is a conscious decision
- Must be approved by appropriate authority
- Must be documented

Accepting risk does **not** mean ignoring it.

---

## 12.8 Escalation During Risk Evaluation

Risks must be escalated when:
- They exceed tolerance
- Controls are ineffective
- Ownership is unclear
- Impact is systemic

Escalation is a governance mechanism, not failure.

---

## 12.9 Risk Evaluation Biases

Common evaluation biases include:
- Optimism bias
- Normalization of deviance
- Anchoring to past outcomes
- Political pressure

Good governance challenges bias.

---

## 12.10 Documenting Risk Evaluation

Risk evaluation records should include:
- Decision rationale
- Acceptance or treatment choice
- Approver
- Review timeline

Documentation protects both the organization and decision-makers.

---

## 12.11 Review and Re-evaluation

Risk evaluation is not permanent.

Re-evaluation is required when:
- Conditions change
- Controls change
- Incidents occur
- New data emerges

Static decisions create hidden exposure.

---

## 12.12 Summary

Risk evaluation:
- Compares risk against criteria
- Enables prioritization
- Drives action or acceptance
- Requires governance and judgment

Evaluation is where leadership responsibility is exercised.
