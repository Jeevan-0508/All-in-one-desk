# Chapter 29: Emerging Risks

---

## 29.1 What Are Emerging Risks

Emerging risks are risks that:
- Are new or evolving
- Have increasing uncertainty
- Are not yet fully understood
- May have significant future impact

They often lack historical data.

---

## 29.2 Why Emerging Risks Matter

Emerging risks matter because:
- They materialize suddenly
- Controls are immature or absent
- Organizations are unprepared
- Impact can be disproportionate

Most major failures start as ignored emerging risks.

---

## 29.3 Characteristics of Emerging Risks

Common characteristics include:
- High uncertainty
- Rapid change
- Cross-domain impact
- Weak or no signals
- Limited ownership

They challenge traditional risk methods.

---

## 29.4 Sources of Emerging Risks

Emerging risks arise from:
- Technological change
- Regulatory evolution
- Geopolitical shifts
- Societal changes
- Market disruption
- Environmental factors

External scanning is essential.

---

## 29.5 Difference Between Emerging and Existing Risks

| Existing Risks | Emerging Risks |
|--------------|----------------|
| Well-understood | Poorly understood |
| Historical data exists | Limited or no data |
| Established controls | Immature controls |
| Stable patterns | Rapid evolution |

Emerging risks require adaptive management.

---

## 29.6 Identifying Emerging Risks

Techniques include:
- Horizon scanning
- Trend analysis
- Expert workshops
- Industry intelligence
- Regulatory monitoring
- Incident pattern analysis

No single method is sufficient.

---

## 29.7 Weak Signals and Early Indicators

Emerging risks often appear as:
- Near misses
- Anomalies
- Small incidents
- Regulatory inquiries
- External events

Weak signals must be taken seriously.

---

## 29.8 Assessing Emerging Risks

Assessment focuses on:
- Direction of change
- Potential impact range
- Speed of emergence
- Areas of exposure

Precision is less important than awareness.

---

## 29.9 Managing Emerging Risks

Management actions may include:
- Increased monitoring
- Scenario analysis
- Pilot controls
- Assigning provisional ownership
- Escalation to governance forums

Early action reduces future cost.

---

## 29.10 Emerging Risks and Risk Appetite

Emerging risks often:
- Sit outside defined appetite
- Require leadership judgment
- Challenge existing tolerance thresholds

Explicit discussion is required.

---

## 29.11 Governance of Emerging Risks

Governance should ensure:
- Regular review of emerging risks
- Leadership visibility
- Cross-functional input
- Decision tracking

Emerging risks must not remain informal.

---

## 29.12 Common Failures in Managing Emerging Risks

Common failures include:
- Waiting for certainty
- Treating them as theoretical
- Lack of ownership
- No monitoring plan

By the time certainty arrives, it’s often too late.

---

## 29.13 Emerging Risks in Practice

Effective organizations:
- Maintain emerging risk registers
- Discuss future threats openly
- Invest early in understanding
- Adjust strategy proactively

Preparedness beats prediction.

---

## 29.14 Summary

Emerging risks:
- Signal future threats
- Require adaptive approaches
- Demand leadership attention
- Drive strategic resilience

Ignoring them creates blind spots.
