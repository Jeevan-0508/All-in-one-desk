# Chapter 17: Risk Appetite

---

## 17.1 What Is Risk Appetite

Risk appetite is the **amount and type of risk an organization is willing to accept**
in pursuit of its objectives.

It defines the boundary between:
- Acceptable risk
- Unacceptable risk

Risk appetite is a strategic decision.

---

## 17.2 Why Risk Appetite Exists

Organizations cannot eliminate all risk.

Risk appetite exists to:
- Enable decision-making
- Guide risk treatment
- Align risk with strategy
- Prevent over-control or under-control

Without appetite, risk decisions become inconsistent.

---

## 17.3 Risk Appetite vs Risk Tolerance

| Concept | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Risk Appetite | Overall willingness to take risk |
| Risk Tolerance | Acceptable variation around objectives |

Risk appetite is directional.  
Risk tolerance is measurable.

---

## 17.4 Risk Appetite Statement

A risk appetite statement formally documents:
- What risks are acceptable
- What risks are restricted
- What risks are prohibited

Example:
> “The organization has low appetite for regulatory breaches,
moderate appetite for operational innovation,
and high appetite for controlled experimentation.”

---

## 17.5 Levels of Risk Appetite

Risk appetite varies by domain:

- Strategic risk → Higher appetite
- Operational risk → Moderate appetite
- Compliance risk → Low appetite
- Safety risk → Zero or near-zero appetite

One appetite does not fit all risks.

---

## 17.6 Risk Appetite and Decision-Making

Risk appetite guides decisions such as:
- Whether to proceed with an initiative
- Whether to accept residual risk
- Whether to invest in controls
- Whether to escalate issues

Decisions without appetite are subjective.

---

## 17.7 Risk Appetite and Governance

Risk appetite must be:
- Approved by leadership
- Communicated across the organization
- Embedded into policies and processes

Governance ensures appetite is applied consistently.

---

## 17.8 Risk Appetite Metrics

Risk appetite should be supported by metrics:
- Loss thresholds
- Incident frequency
- Compliance breach tolerance
- Service disruption limits

Metrics turn appetite into action.

---

## 17.9 Breaching Risk Appetite

When risk exceeds appetite:
- Escalation is mandatory
- Decisions must be revisited
- Additional controls may be required

Operating outside appetite is a governance failure.

---

## 17.10 Risk Appetite Review

Risk appetite must be reviewed when:
- Strategy changes
- External environment shifts
- Major incidents occur
- Regulatory expectations evolve

Static appetite leads to blind spots.

---

## 17.11 Common Failures in Risk Appetite

Common failures include:
- Appetite not documented
- Appetite too vague
- Appetite ignored in practice
- Appetite not linked to metrics

Declared appetite without enforcement is meaningless.

---

## 17.12 Risk Appetite vs Risk Culture

Risk appetite sets boundaries.  
Risk culture determines behavior within those boundaries.

Both must align for effective risk management.

---

## 17.13 Summary

Risk appetite:
- Defines acceptable risk
- Aligns risk with strategy
- Enables consistent decisions
- Requires governance and metrics

Without risk appetite, risk management is reactive.
