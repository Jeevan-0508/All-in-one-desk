# Chapter 32: Risk Lifecycle Summary

---

## 32.1 Purpose of the Risk Lifecycle

The risk lifecycle provides a structured,
repeatable approach to managing risk
from identification through closure.

It ensures risks are not managed in isolation.

---

## 32.2 Overview of the Risk Lifecycle

The lifecycle consists of:

1. Context Establishment  
2. Risk Identification  
3. Risk Assessment  
4. Risk Analysis  
5. Risk Evaluation  
6. Risk Treatment  
7. Residual Risk Assessment  
8. Monitoring and Reporting  
9. Escalation and Review  
10. Closure or Reassessment  

Risk management is continuous.

---

## 32.3 Context Establishment

Context includes:
- Organizational objectives
- Risk appetite and tolerance
- Regulatory environment
- Stakeholders
- Operational scope

Context defines relevance.

---

## 32.4 Risk Identification

Risks are identified through:
- Process analysis
- Incident reviews
- Scenario analysis
- Emerging risk scanning
- Third-party assessments

Identification must be ongoing.

---

## 32.5 Risk Assessment

Assessment determines:
- Likelihood
- Impact
- Velocity
- Detectability

Both qualitative and quantitative methods are used.

---

## 32.6 Risk Analysis

Analysis explores:
- Root causes
- Interdependencies
- Control effectiveness
- Risk aggregation

Understanding drives decisions.

---

## 32.7 Risk Evaluation

Evaluation compares risk exposure
against defined thresholds.

Outcomes include:
- Accept
- Treat
- Escalate
- Transfer

Evaluation is a governance step.

---

## 32.8 Risk Prioritization

Prioritization ensures:
- Focus on critical risks
- Efficient resource allocation
- Timely action

Not all risks receive equal attention.

---

## 32.9 Risk Treatment

Treatment options include:
- Avoidance
- Mitigation
- Transfer
- Acceptance

Controls must be proportional.

---

## 32.10 Residual Risk

Residual risk remains after treatment.

Organizations must:
- Assess acceptability
- Monitor closely
- Revisit when context changes

Zero risk does not exist.

---

## 32.11 Monitoring and Reporting

Monitoring includes:
- KRIs
- KPIs
- Incident trends
- Control performance

Reporting enables oversight.

---

## 32.12 Escalation

Escalation occurs when:
- Risk exceeds appetite
- Controls fail
- Conditions change rapidly

Timely escalation prevents loss.

---

## 32.13 Risk Closure

Risks may be closed when:
- Threat no longer exists
- Exposure is fully mitigated
- Activity ends

Closure must be documented.

---

## 32.14 Continuous Improvement

Risk management improves through:
- Lessons learned
- Incident analysis
- Maturity assessments
- Feedback loops

The framework evolves.

---

## 32.15 Integration Across Domains

The lifecycle applies to:
- Operational risk
- Cyber risk
- Third-party risk
- Program risk
- Compliance risk

One lifecycle, many contexts.

---

## 32.16 Governance and Accountability

Effective governance ensures:
- Clear ownership
- Leadership oversight
- Decision traceability

Governance sustains discipline.

---

## 32.17 Common Breakpoints in the Lifecycle

Failures often occur due to:
- Weak identification
- Poor escalation
- Infrequent monitoring
- Documentation-only approach

Awareness prevents breakdown.

---

## 32.18 Summary

The risk lifecycle:
- Connects all risk activities
- Enables proactive management
- Supports informed decisions
- Protects organizational objectives

It is the backbone of risk management.
