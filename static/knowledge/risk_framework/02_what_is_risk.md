# Chapter 02: What Is Risk
## 2.1 Formal Definition of Risk
The internationally accepted definition of risk (ISO 31000) is:
> **Risk is the effect of uncertainty on objectives.**
This definition is intentionally broad and applies to:
- Business objectives
- Operational objectives
- Financial objectives
- Security objectives
- Compliance objectives
Key takeaway:
Risk only exists **in relation to an objective**.
If there is no objective, there is no risk.

## 2.2 Simple Definition of Risk
In simple, practical terms:
> **Risk is the possibility that something may not go as planned.**
This definition helps operational teams understand risk without jargon.
Both definitions are valid and used at different levels:
- Formal definition → governance, standards
- Simple definition → daily operations

## 2.3 Core Components of Risk
Every risk has **three fundamental components**:

1. **Cause**
2. **Event**
3. **Impact**
### Example:
- Cause: Weak access controls
- Event: Unauthorized system access
- Impact: Data breach, financial loss, reputational damage
Without all three components, risk is not fully defined.

## 2.4 Risk vs Uncertainty
| Concept | Uncertainty | Risk |
|------|-------------|------|
| Definition | Unknown future outcomes | Uncertainty with impact |
| Measurable | Often not measurable | Can be assessed |
| Actionable | Limited | Yes |
Uncertainty becomes **risk** when:
- An objective exists
- A potential impact exists

## 2.5 Positive and Negative Risk
Risk is not always negative.
### Negative Risk (Threat)
- System failure
- Fraud
- Data breach
- Supply disruption
### Positive Risk (Opportunity)
- Faster delivery
- Cost optimization
- Process improvement
- Innovation gains
Effective risk management:
- Reduces threats
- Enables opportunities

## 2.6 Risk Is Not an Incident
A common mistake is confusing **risk** with **incident**.
| Risk | Incident |
|----|---------|
| Future possibility | Past or current event |
| Preventable | Already occurred |
| Managed through controls | Managed through response |
Example:
- Risk: Possibility of system outage
- Incident: System outage occurred at 2 AM
Risk management focuses on the **future**.

## 2.7 Risk Exists in Systems, Not Isolation
Risks rarely occur alone.
They:
- Cascade
- Compound
- Amplify each other
Example:
- Cyber risk → Operational disruption → Reputational damage → Financial loss
Understanding **risk interdependencies** is critical.

## 2.8 Risk Perception vs Actual Risk
Risk perception varies based on:
- Experience
- Role
- Incentives
- Past incidents
Actual risk is based on:
- Likelihood
- Impact
- Controls effectiveness
Good frameworks separate:
- Emotional response
- Data-driven assessment

## 2.9 Why Risk Must Be Explicit
Unidentified risks:
- Are unmanaged
- Are invisible to leadership
- Recur repeatedly
Documenting risk:
- Creates accountability
- Enables mitigation
- Supports learning

Undocumented risk is **implicit risk**.

## 2.10 Risk as a Management Responsibility
Risk is not owned by risk teams alone.
- Business owns risk
- Risk teams support and challenge
- Leadership sets boundaries
Risk management is a **management discipline**, not a support function.

## 2.11 Summary
Key points:
- Risk exists only where objectives exist
- Risk has cause, event, and impact
- Risk is future-focused
- Risk can be positive or negative
- Risk must be explicit to be managed
