# Chapter 16: Residual Risk

---

## 16.1 What Is Residual Risk

Residual risk is the level of risk that **remains after all risk treatment and controls have been applied**.

It represents:
- What cannot be eliminated
- What must be tolerated
- What leadership ultimately accepts

Residual risk is unavoidable.

---

## 16.2 Inherent Risk vs Residual Risk (Revisited)

| Aspect | Inherent Risk | Residual Risk |
|-----|---------------|---------------|
| Timing | Before controls | After controls |
| Nature | Raw exposure | Remaining exposure |
| Control impact | None applied | Controls considered |

Controls exist to reduce inherent risk to an acceptable residual level.

---

## 16.3 Why Residual Risk Always Exists

Residual risk exists because:
- Controls are imperfect
- Humans make mistakes
- Systems fail
- Environments change
- Resources are limited

Zero risk is unrealistic outside of stopping all activity.

---

## 16.4 Residual Risk and Leadership Accountability

Residual risk is **a leadership decision**, not a technical one.

Leadership must decide:
- Whether remaining exposure is acceptable
- Whether further mitigation is justified
- Whether objectives should be adjusted

Risk teams inform — leaders decide.

---

## 16.5 Acceptable vs Unacceptable Residual Risk

Residual risk must be compared against:
- Risk appetite
- Risk tolerance
- Regulatory expectations

Unacceptable residual risk requires:
- Additional controls
- Risk avoidance
- Escalation

Ignoring residual risk is silent acceptance.

---

## 16.6 Residual Risk Monitoring

Residual risk must be:
- Continuously monitored
- Reviewed periodically
- Reassessed when conditions change

Static acceptance leads to surprise failures.

---

## 16.7 Common Misunderstandings About Residual Risk

Common myths include:
- “Controls eliminate risk”
- “Low score means no risk”
- “Residual risk can be ignored”

Residual risk must be actively managed.

---

## 16.8 Residual Risk Documentation

Residual risk documentation should include:
- Residual risk level
- Controls in place
- Acceptance rationale
- Approver
- Review frequency

Documentation creates accountability.

---

## 16.9 Residual Risk and Audit Expectations

Auditors focus on:
- Whether residual risk is identified
- Whether acceptance is authorized
- Whether monitoring exists

Undocumented acceptance is a control failure.

---

## 16.10 Residual Risk Over Time

Residual risk is not static.

It can:
- Increase as controls degrade
- Decrease as controls mature
- Change with environment

Regular reassessment is mandatory.

---

## 16.11 Summary

Residual risk:
- Is unavoidable
- Requires leadership ownership
- Must align with appetite
- Requires monitoring and review

Managing residual risk is a core governance responsibility.
