# Chapter 33: Case Studies

---

## 33.1 Purpose of Case Studies

Case studies demonstrate:
- How risks materialize
- How controls succeed or fail
- How decisions impact outcomes

They convert theory into judgment.

---

## 33.2 Case Study 1: Operational Risk – Process Failure

### Scenario
A critical operational process relies on a manual approval step.
Due to staff shortage, approvals are delayed.

### Identified Risks
- Operational risk
- Customer impact risk
- Reputational risk

### Root Causes
- Single-point dependency
- No backup resources
- Lack of automation

### Mitigation Actions
- Cross-training staff
- Automating approvals
- Defining escalation timelines

### Lessons Learned
Manual controls do not scale.

---

## 33.3 Case Study 2: Cyber Risk – Third-Party Breach

### Scenario
A vendor with system access suffers a cyber breach,
leading to unauthorized data exposure.

### Identified Risks
- Cybersecurity risk
- Third-party risk
- Compliance risk

### Control Gaps
- Weak vendor security assessment
- No continuous monitoring
- Delayed incident notification

### Mitigation Actions
- Strengthened vendor onboarding
- Contractual security clauses
- Continuous risk reassessment

### Lessons Learned
Risk is shared, accountability is not.

---

## 33.4 Case Study 3: Compliance Risk – Regulatory Change

### Scenario
A new regulation introduces stricter reporting timelines.
The organization fails to adapt processes in time.

### Identified Risks
- Compliance risk
- Financial penalties
- Reputational damage

### Root Causes
- Poor regulatory monitoring
- No ownership assigned
- Slow change management

### Mitigation Actions
- Regulatory horizon scanning
- Clear compliance ownership
- Early impact assessments

### Lessons Learned
Late awareness equals non-compliance.

---

## 33.5 Case Study 4: Program Risk – Dependency Failure

### Scenario
A major transformation program depends on
multiple upstream system changes.
One dependency is delayed.

### Identified Risks
- Program risk
- Schedule risk
- Benefit realization risk

### Root Causes
- Weak dependency mapping
- Optimism bias
- No contingency planning

### Mitigation Actions
- Dependency risk registers
- Milestone-based escalation
- Buffer planning

### Lessons Learned
Dependencies multiply risk.

---

## 33.6 Case Study 5: Emerging Risk – Technology Shift

### Scenario
A new technology is adopted rapidly
without full understanding of implications.

### Identified Risks
- Emerging risk
- Cyber risk
- Operational risk

### Control Challenges
- No historical data
- Immature controls
- Skill gaps

### Mitigation Actions
- Pilot implementations
- Enhanced monitoring
- Expert involvement

### Lessons Learned
Speed without control creates exposure.

---

## 33.7 Case Study 6: Reputational Risk – Public Incident

### Scenario
An operational failure becomes public via social media.

### Identified Risks
- Reputational risk
- Customer trust erosion
- Brand damage

### Response Gaps
- Slow communication
- Unclear ownership
- Inconsistent messaging

### Mitigation Actions
- Crisis response playbooks
- Clear communication ownership
- Simulation exercises

### Lessons Learned
Reputation moves faster than controls.

---

## 33.8 Patterns Across Case Studies

Common themes include:
- Early signals ignored
- Ownership unclear
- Overconfidence in controls
- Weak escalation

Patterns matter more than incidents.

---

## 33.9 Using Case Studies in Risk Management

Case studies should be used to:
- Train teams
- Test frameworks
- Improve decision-making
- Challenge assumptions

They are learning tools.

---

## 33.10 Summary

Case studies show that:
- Risk is predictable
- Failures are rarely sudden
- Controls must evolve
- Judgment matters

Experience can be simulated.
