# Chapter 24: Risk Register

---

## 24.1 What Is a Risk Register

A risk register is a **centralized, structured record**
of identified risks within an organization.

It captures:
- What the risk is
- Where it exists
- Who owns it
- How it is managed
- Its current status

The risk register is the system of record for risk.

---

## 24.2 Purpose of a Risk Register

The risk register exists to:
- Provide visibility into risk exposure
- Support prioritization
- Enable tracking and escalation
- Support governance and reporting
- Create audit evidence

Unrecorded risks are unmanaged risks.

---

## 24.3 Risk Register vs Risk List

| Risk List | Risk Register |
|---------|---------------|
| Informal | Formal |
| Static | Living document |
| No ownership | Defined ownership |
| No lifecycle | Lifecycle tracking |

A list identifies risk.  
A register manages risk.

---

## 24.4 Core Elements of a Risk Register

A standard risk register includes:

- Risk ID
- Risk title
- Risk description
- Risk category
- Root cause
- Impact
- Likelihood
- Inherent risk rating
- Controls in place
- Residual risk rating
- Risk owner
- Control owners
- Risk status
- Mitigation actions
- Review date

Consistency is critical.

---

## 24.5 Risk Categorization in the Register

Risks should be categorized to:
- Enable aggregation
- Identify trends
- Support reporting

Common categories:
- Strategic
- Operational
- Financial
- Compliance
- Cybersecurity
- Reputational

---

## 24.6 Risk Scoring in the Register

The register records:
- Inherent risk (before controls)
- Residual risk (after controls)

Scoring supports:
- Prioritization
- Escalation
- Resource allocation

---

## 24.7 Ownership and Accountability

Each risk must have:
- One risk owner
- Clearly defined control owners

Ownership ensures accountability and action.

---

## 24.8 Risk Status Tracking

Risk status reflects current posture:
- Open
- Mitigated
- Accepted
- Escalated
- Closed

Status must be updated regularly.

---

## 24.9 Risk Register as a Living Artifact

A risk register must be:
- Reviewed periodically
- Updated after incidents
- Adjusted as conditions change

Static registers provide false assurance.

---

## 24.10 Risk Register and Governance

The risk register feeds:
- Risk committees
- Executive reporting
- Board oversight
- Audit reviews

It enables informed governance decisions.

---

## 24.11 Risk Register and Escalation

Escalation is triggered when:
- Residual risk exceeds tolerance
- Controls fail
- Impact or likelihood increases

The register records escalation actions.

---

## 24.12 Tools and Formats

Risk registers may exist in:
- Spreadsheets
- GRC tools
- Databases
- Integrated dashboards

The format matters less than discipline.

---

## 24.13 Common Failures in Risk Registers

Common failures include:
- Incomplete entries
- No ownership
- No updates
- Overly complex fields
- Treated as a compliance artifact

A neglected register is worse than none.

---

## 24.14 Risk Register in Practice

Effective organizations:
- Keep registers concise
- Review them routinely
- Use them for decisions
- Integrate them with reporting

The register should drive action.

---

## 24.15 Summary

The risk register:
- Centralizes risk information
- Enables prioritization and escalation
- Supports governance and audit
- Turns risk management into execution

Without a risk register, risk management is theoretical.
