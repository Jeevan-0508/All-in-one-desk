# Chapter 11: Risk Analysis Methods

---

## 11.1 What Is Risk Analysis

Risk analysis is the process of:
- Deeply understanding how a risk unfolds
- Examining causes, pathways, and consequences
- Evaluating control effectiveness in context

Risk analysis goes beyond scoring.
It explains **why a risk exists and how it materializes**.

---

## 11.2 Risk Analysis vs Risk Assessment

| Aspect | Risk Assessment | Risk Analysis |
|-----|-----------------|--------------|
| Focus | Severity & priority | Structure & behavior |
| Output | Scores | Insights |
| Depth | High-level | Detailed |

Analysis informs **better treatment decisions**.

---

## 11.3 When Risk Analysis Is Required

Risk analysis is most useful when:
- Risks are high-impact
- Systems are complex
- Failures cascade
- Regulatory scrutiny exists
- Decisions are irreversible

Not every risk requires deep analysis.

---

## 11.4 Scenario Analysis

Scenario analysis explores:
- Plausible future risk events
- How risks may evolve
- Worst-case and best-case outcomes

Steps:
1. Define scenario
2. Identify triggers
3. Map impacts
4. Assess controls
5. Estimate consequences

Used extensively for:
- Cyber risk
- Supply chain disruption
- Crisis management

---

## 11.5 Root Cause Analysis (RCA)

RCA identifies:
- Underlying causes of risk
- Systemic weaknesses

Common RCA techniques:
- 5 Whys
- Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram
- Fault tree analysis

RCA prevents recurrence.

---

## 11.6 Failure Modes and Effects Analysis (FMEA)

FMEA analyzes:
- How processes fail
- Severity of failure
- Likelihood of occurrence
- Detectability

Typical steps:
1. Identify failure modes
2. Assess severity
3. Assess occurrence
4. Assess detection
5. Calculate risk priority

Common in:
- Operations
- Manufacturing
- Process-heavy environments

---

## 11.7 Bow-Tie Analysis

Bow-tie analysis visualizes:
- Causes on the left
- Risk event in the center
- Consequences on the right
- Controls on both sides

It shows:
- Preventive controls
- Detective controls
- Mitigating controls

Bow-tie is excellent for communication.

---

## 11.8 Fault Tree Analysis (FTA)

FTA models:
- Logical combinations of failures
- How multiple issues combine to cause an event

Uses:
- AND / OR logic gates
- Probability calculations

Common in:
- Engineering
- Safety-critical systems

---

## 11.9 Event Tree Analysis (ETA)

ETA explores:
- Outcomes after an initiating event
- Effectiveness of response controls

It helps evaluate:
- Incident response
- Recovery capabilities

---

## 11.10 Stress Testing

Stress testing evaluates:
- System behavior under extreme conditions
- Control robustness
- Breakpoints

Used for:
- Financial risk
- Capacity planning
- Cyber resilience

---

## 11.11 Data-Driven Risk Analysis

Data-based analysis uses:
- Incident trends
- Loss data
- Operational metrics
- Anomaly detection

It improves objectivity but requires:
- Clean data
- Contextual interpretation

---

## 11.12 Choosing the Right Method

Method selection depends on:
- Risk criticality
- Complexity
- Available data
- Time constraints
- Stakeholder needs

Over-analysis wastes effort.
Under-analysis creates blind spots.

---

## 11.13 Documentation and Outputs

Risk analysis outputs may include:
- Scenario narratives
- Diagrams and models
- Assumptions and limitations
- Control recommendations

Documentation ensures transparency.

---

## 11.14 Summary

Risk analysis:
- Explains risk behavior
- Improves treatment decisions
- Supports resilience
- Enables informed leadership decisions

Analysis depth should match risk criticality.
