# Chapter 08: Risk Identification

---

## 8.1 What Is Risk Identification

Risk identification is the **systematic process of discovering potential events**
that could affect the achievement of objectives.

It focuses on:
- What could go wrong
- Why it could happen
- Where exposure exists

Risk identification is the **foundation** of the entire risk lifecycle.
Unidentified risks cannot be assessed, treated, or monitored.

---

## 8.2 Objectives of Risk Identification

The objectives are to:
- Create visibility of uncertainty
- Detect risks early
- Prevent surprises
- Enable proactive mitigation

Good identification aims for **coverage**, not perfection.

---

## 8.3 When Risk Identification Should Occur

Risk identification is not a one-time activity.

It should occur:
- During planning
- During execution
- After incidents
- When changes occur
- Periodically as part of reviews

Change is the biggest risk trigger.

---

## 8.4 Common Triggers for Risk Identification

Triggers include:
- New processes or systems
- Regulatory changes
- Vendor onboarding
- Volume spikes
- Incidents or near-misses
- Technology changes
- Organizational restructuring

Every change introduces new risk.

---

## 8.5 Sources of Risk

Risks originate from multiple sources:

### Internal Sources
- People
- Processes
- Systems
- Data
- Governance

### External Sources
- Vendors
- Customers
- Regulators
- Market conditions
- Geopolitical factors

Understanding sources prevents superficial identification.

---

## 8.6 Top-Down Risk Identification

Top-down approaches focus on:
- Strategic objectives
- Leadership perspective
- Enterprise-wide exposure

Methods include:
- Executive workshops
- Strategic reviews
- Scenario planning

Top-down views capture **big-picture risks**.

---

## 8.7 Bottom-Up Risk Identification

Bottom-up approaches focus on:
- Operational processes
- Day-to-day activities
- Frontline experience

Methods include:
- Process walkthroughs
- Team brainstorming
- Incident reviews
- Control testing

Bottom-up views capture **real-world risk**.

---

## 8.8 Common Risk Identification Techniques

Widely used techniques include:
- Brainstorming sessions
- Checklists
- Process mapping
- Interviews
- Surveys
- Incident analysis
- Audit findings
- Data trend analysis

No single method is sufficient alone.

---

## 8.9 Using Risk Taxonomy in Identification

Risk taxonomy ensures:
- Consistent categorization
- Complete coverage
- Comparable results

Each identified risk should map to:
- A risk category
- A subcategory
- A specific driver

Taxonomy prevents duplication and gaps.

---

## 8.10 Identifying Root Causes, Not Symptoms

Effective identification focuses on:
- Underlying causes
- Control weaknesses
- Systemic issues

Example:
- Symptom: Late deliveries
- Root cause: Capacity planning failure

Treating symptoms hides risk.

---

## 8.11 Avoiding Common Identification Pitfalls

Common mistakes include:
- Only identifying obvious risks
- Copying old risk registers
- Ignoring near-misses
- Overlooking human factors
- Focusing only on compliance

Risk identification must be curious and honest.

---

## 8.12 Documenting Identified Risks

Each risk should be documented with:
- Clear description
- Cause, event, impact
- Category
- Owner (if known)

Poor documentation leads to confusion later.

---

## 8.13 Risk Identification Outputs

Typical outputs include:
- Risk register entries
- Heat maps (after assessment)
- Action lists
- Escalation items

Identification feeds directly into assessment.

---

## 8.14 Summary

Effective risk identification:
- Is continuous
- Uses multiple techniques
- Leverages taxonomy
- Focuses on causes
- Involves the business

Missed risks are unmanaged risks.
