# Chapter 13: Risk Prioritization

---

## 13.1 What Is Risk Prioritization

Risk prioritization is the process of:
- Ordering evaluated risks
- Deciding focus areas
- Allocating limited resources

Not all risks can be treated at once.
Prioritization ensures effort is applied where it matters most.

---

## 13.2 Why Prioritization Is Necessary

Organizations face:
- Limited budgets
- Limited time
- Limited attention

Without prioritization:
- Teams chase low-impact risks
- Critical risks remain untreated
- Decision-makers are overwhelmed

Prioritization converts analysis into **actionable focus**.

---

## 13.3 Risk Prioritization vs Risk Evaluation

| Aspect | Risk Evaluation | Risk Prioritization |
|-----|-----------------|--------------------|
| Question | Is this acceptable? | What comes first? |
| Output | Decision category | Ordered list |
| Scope | Individual risk | Risk portfolio |

Evaluation judges risk.
Prioritization manages risk **collectively**.

---

## 13.4 Inputs to Risk Prioritization

Key inputs include:
- Residual risk severity
- Risk velocity
- Interdependencies
- Control gaps
- Strategic alignment
- Regulatory sensitivity

Prioritization must consider **context**, not just scores.

---

## 13.5 Risk Velocity and Urgency

Risk velocity refers to:
- How quickly impact materializes after occurrence

Fast-moving risks require:
- Immediate attention
- Strong preventive controls

Slow-moving risks may allow:
- Planned mitigation
- Monitoring-based response

Velocity changes prioritization order.

---

## 13.6 Risk Interdependencies and Cascading Effects

Some risks trigger others.

Example:
- Cyber breach → Operational shutdown → Reputational damage → Financial loss

Prioritization must consider:
- Root risks
- Amplifying risks
- Downstream impacts

Managing root risks yields higher leverage.

---

## 13.7 Strategic and Regulatory Considerations

Certain risks receive priority due to:
- Regulatory mandates
- Legal consequences
- Strategic objectives
- Public visibility

A medium-scored regulatory risk may outrank a high operational risk.

---

## 13.8 Risk Concentration and Aggregation

Multiple small risks may:
- Accumulate
- Concentrate in one area
- Create systemic exposure

Prioritization should identify:
- Risk clusters
- Concentration points

Aggregated risk is often underestimated.

---

## 13.9 Portfolio View of Risk

Risk should be viewed as a portfolio, not a list.

Portfolio views enable:
- Balance between risk types
- Trade-offs between areas
- Strategic alignment

This perspective supports leadership decision-making.

---

## 13.10 Common Prioritization Pitfalls

Common mistakes include:
- Blind reliance on scores
- Political influence
- Overreaction to recent incidents
- Ignoring slow-burn risks

Good prioritization resists bias.

---

## 13.11 Communicating Risk Priorities

Priorities must be:
- Clearly communicated
- Understood by stakeholders
- Linked to action plans

Unclear priorities result in misaligned execution.

---

## 13.12 Reviewing and Updating Priorities

Risk priorities must be revisited when:
- Conditions change
- Incidents occur
- Controls improve or degrade
- Strategy shifts

Static prioritization creates blind spots.

---

## 13.13 Summary

Risk prioritization:
- Directs focus
- Optimizes resource use
- Considers severity, velocity, and context
- Enables portfolio-level risk management

Effective prioritization is a leadership capability.
