# Chapter 09: Risk Sources

---

## 9.1 What Are Risk Sources

Risk sources are the **origins or drivers** that create uncertainty
and give rise to risk events.

They explain:
- Why risks exist
- How risks originate
- Why similar risks repeat

Understanding sources is essential to designing **effective controls**.

---

## 9.2 Risk Sources vs Risk Events

| Element | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Risk Source | Origin of uncertainty |
| Risk Event | What actually happens |
| Impact | Consequence of the event |

Example:
- Source: Weak access governance
- Event: Unauthorized system access
- Impact: Data breach

Treating events without addressing sources results in recurring incidents.

---

## 9.3 Internal Risk Sources

Internal sources originate **within the organization’s control**.

They are often the most actionable.

---

## 9.4 People-Related Risk Sources

Human-related sources include:
- Lack of training
- Fatigue and burnout
- Poor judgment
- Intentional misconduct
- Role ambiguity
- Incentive misalignment

People are both:
- The strongest control
- The biggest source of risk

---

## 9.5 Process-Related Risk Sources

Process risks arise from:
- Undefined processes
- Manual steps
- Excessive complexity
- Poor documentation
- Lack of standardization

Examples:
- Inconsistent approvals
- Bypassed controls
- Dependency on individuals

Weak processes amplify human error.

---

## 9.6 Technology and System Risk Sources

Technology risks arise from:
- System failures
- Legacy platforms
- Poor integrations
- Inadequate monitoring
- Weak access controls

Technology introduces **scale and speed** to risk.

---

## 9.7 Data-Related Risk Sources

Data risks arise from:
- Poor data quality
- Incomplete data
- Unauthorized access
- Data silos
- Incorrect assumptions

Bad data leads to bad decisions.

---

## 9.8 Governance and Control Weaknesses

Governance risks include:
- Undefined ownership
- Weak escalation paths
- Ineffective policies
- Inconsistent enforcement

Strong controls on paper but weak governance
create a false sense of safety.

---

## 9.9 External Risk Sources

External sources are **outside direct control** but must still be managed.

---

## 9.10 Third-Party and Vendor Risk Sources

Third-party risks arise from:
- Outsourcing critical functions
- Vendor security posture
- Contractual gaps
- Dependency concentration

Organizations inherit vendor risks.

---

## 9.11 Regulatory and Legal Risk Sources

Regulatory risks arise from:
- Changing laws
- Jurisdictional differences
- Ambiguous requirements
- Enforcement actions

Compliance risk often lags behind regulatory change.

---

## 9.12 Market and Economic Risk Sources

Market risks include:
- Demand fluctuations
- Cost volatility
- Competitive pressure
- Inflation
- Currency changes

These risks affect strategic planning.

---

## 9.13 Environmental and Geopolitical Risk Sources

Examples include:
- Natural disasters
- Political instability
- Trade restrictions
- Pandemics

These risks can cascade across supply chains.

---

## 9.14 Risk Source Interactions

Risk sources interact and compound.

Example:
- Vendor dependency (external)
- Weak governance (internal)
- Poor monitoring (technology)

Together they create systemic risk.

---

## 9.15 Mapping Risk Sources to Risk Categories

Each risk source should map to:
- Risk taxonomy category
- Specific risk events
- Controls and mitigations

Source-based mapping enables **root-cause mitigation**.

---

## 9.16 Summary

Risk sources:
- Explain why risks exist
- Enable targeted mitigation
- Prevent repeated incidents
- Support proactive risk management

Understanding sources is more powerful than listing events.
