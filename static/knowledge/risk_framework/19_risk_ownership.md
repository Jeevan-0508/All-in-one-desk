# Chapter 19: Risk Ownership

---

## 19.1 What Is Risk Ownership

Risk ownership is the assignment of **clear accountability**
for managing a specific risk.

A risk owner:
- Understands the risk
- Ensures controls exist
- Accepts residual risk
- Initiates escalation when needed

Ownership cannot be shared or implied.

---

## 19.2 Why Risk Ownership Matters

Without ownership:
- Risks remain unmanaged
- Issues are repeatedly discussed
- Escalations fail
- Accountability dissolves

Every risk must have one owner.

---

## 19.3 Risk Owner vs Control Owner

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Risk Owner | Overall risk accountability |
| Control Owner | Day-to-day control execution |

One risk may have multiple controls,
but only one risk owner.

---

## 19.4 Who Can Be a Risk Owner

Risk owners are typically:
- Business leaders
- Process owners
- Senior managers

Risk cannot be owned by audit or compliance alone.

---

## 19.5 Responsibilities of a Risk Owner

Risk owners must:
- Review risk regularly
- Ensure controls are effective
- Approve residual risk
- Trigger escalation when tolerance is breached
- Ensure documentation is accurate

Ownership requires authority.

---

## 19.6 Risk Ownership Across the Lifecycle

Risk ownership applies at every stage:
- Identification
- Assessment
- Treatment
- Monitoring
- Review

Ownership does not end after documentation.

---

## 19.7 Escalation and Risk Ownership

Risk owners must escalate when:
- Risk exceeds tolerance
- Controls fail
- Impact increases
- Uncertainty grows

Failure to escalate is a governance failure.

---

## 19.8 Risk Ownership and Governance

Governance frameworks ensure:
- Owners are assigned
- Owners are accountable
- Decisions are documented

Ownership without governance is symbolic.

---

## 19.9 Common Failures in Risk Ownership

Common failures include:
- Assigning ownership to junior staff
- Unclear authority
- Multiple owners
- Passive ownership

Risk without an owner is unmanaged.

---

## 19.10 Risk Ownership in Practice

Effective ownership includes:
- Named individual
- Role clarity
- Authority alignment
- Performance accountability

Ownership must be visible.

---

## 19.11 Summary

Risk ownership:
- Establishes accountability
- Enables effective control
- Drives escalation
- Supports governance

Without ownership, risk management fails.
