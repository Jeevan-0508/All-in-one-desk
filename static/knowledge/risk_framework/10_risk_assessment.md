# Chapter 10: Risk Assessment

---

## 10.1 What Is Risk Assessment

Risk assessment is the process of:
- Understanding the nature of a risk
- Estimating how likely it is to occur
- Evaluating the potential impact if it occurs

Risk assessment answers:
> “How bad could this be, and how likely is it?”

It transforms identified risks into **decision-ready information**.

---

## 10.2 Risk Assessment vs Risk Identification

| Aspect | Identification | Assessment |
|-----|----------------|------------|
| Focus | What could happen | How serious it is |
| Output | Risk description | Risk severity |
| Nature | Qualitative | Qualitative + Quantitative |

Assessment cannot occur without identification.

---

## 10.3 Objectives of Risk Assessment

The objectives are to:
- Prioritize risks
- Allocate resources effectively
- Support decision-making
- Enable consistent treatment

Not all risks deserve equal attention.

---

## 10.4 Core Dimensions of Risk Assessment

Risk is commonly assessed across two primary dimensions:

1. **Likelihood** – probability of occurrence  
2. **Impact** – severity of consequences

Some frameworks add a third:
- **Velocity** – speed at which impact occurs

---

## 10.5 Likelihood Assessment

Likelihood reflects:
- Frequency of occurrence
- Historical trends
- Control effectiveness
- Exposure duration

Likelihood may be assessed using:
- Qualitative scales (Low / Medium / High)
- Semi-quantitative scales (1–5)
- Quantitative probabilities (percentage)

---

## 10.6 Impact Assessment

Impact measures the **consequence** if the risk materializes.

Impact dimensions may include:
- Financial loss
- Operational disruption
- Regulatory impact
- Reputational damage
- Customer harm
- Safety impact

Impact should be assessed **per dimension**, not as a single vague score.

---

## 10.7 Inherent Risk vs Residual Risk

### Inherent Risk
- Risk level **before controls**
- Reflects raw exposure

### Residual Risk
- Risk level **after controls**
- Reflects remaining exposure

Controls exist to reduce inherent risk to an acceptable residual level.

---

## 10.8 Control Effectiveness

Assessing control effectiveness is critical.

Controls may:
- Prevent risk occurrence
- Detect risk events
- Reduce impact

Control effectiveness depends on:
- Design adequacy
- Consistent execution
- Monitoring

Weak controls distort risk assessment.

---

## 10.9 Qualitative Risk Assessment

Qualitative assessment uses:
- Descriptive scales
- Expert judgment

Advantages:
- Simple
- Fast
- Easy to communicate

Limitations:
- Subjective
- Inconsistent across assessors

Best suited for early-stage or low-maturity environments.

---

## 10.10 Quantitative Risk Assessment

Quantitative assessment uses:
- Numeric values
- Financial modeling
- Statistical methods

Advantages:
- Objective
- Comparable
- Supports cost-benefit analysis

Limitations:
- Data intensive
- Complex
- Assumption-sensitive

Used for high-impact or critical risks.

---

## 10.11 Risk Matrices and Heat Maps

Risk matrices visualize:
- Likelihood vs Impact
- Relative risk severity

Heat maps:
- Aid prioritization
- Support executive communication

They should guide discussion, not replace judgment.

---

## 10.12 Common Risk Assessment Pitfalls

Common errors include:
- Overconfidence in estimates
- Ignoring uncertainty
- Inflated scoring to gain attention
- Understated scoring to avoid escalation
- Treating scores as absolute truth

Assessment supports decisions — it does not make them.

---

## 10.13 Consistency and Calibration

To ensure consistency:
- Use common definitions
- Train assessors
- Calibrate scoring across teams
- Review assessments periodically

Inconsistent assessment undermines credibility.

---

## 10.14 Documentation of Risk Assessment

Risk assessments should be documented with:
- Assumptions
- Rationale
- Data sources
- Control considerations

This supports transparency and auditability.

---

## 10.15 Summary

Risk assessment:
- Prioritizes risks
- Informs decisions
- Considers likelihood, impact, and controls
- Requires judgment and discipline

Assessment quality determines treatment quality.
