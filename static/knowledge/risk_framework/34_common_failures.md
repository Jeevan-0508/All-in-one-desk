# Chapter 34: Common Failures in Risk Management

---

## 34.1 Why Risk Frameworks Fail

Most failures are not due to
lack of frameworks,
but failure in execution.

Risk fails quietly before it fails loudly.

---

## 34.2 Failure 1: Treating Risk as Documentation

### Description
Risk management becomes a compliance exercise.
Documents are created but not used.

### Indicators
- Risk registers not reviewed
- No follow-up on mitigations
- Copy-paste risks

### Impact
- False sense of security
- Missed early warning signals

---

## 34.3 Failure 2: Poor Risk Identification

### Description
Risks are identified too narrowly or too late.

### Indicators
- Focus only on historical risks
- Ignoring near-misses
- No scenario thinking

### Impact
- Blind spots
- Reactive responses

---

## 34.4 Failure 3: Weak Ownership

### Description
Risk ownership is unclear or symbolic.

### Indicators
- Owners without authority
- Shared ownership ambiguity
- No accountability

### Impact
- Delayed action
- Escalation failures

---

## 34.5 Failure 4: Inadequate Risk Assessment

### Description
Assessments lack rigor or consistency.

### Indicators
- Subjective scoring
- No impact definitions
- Likelihood not evidence-based

### Impact
- Mis-prioritized risks
- Resource misallocation

---

## 34.6 Failure 5: Overconfidence in Controls

### Description
Controls are assumed effective without validation.

### Indicators
- No control testing
- Assumptions based on design
- No failure scenarios considered

### Impact
- Control illusion
- Increased residual risk

---

## 34.7 Failure 6: No Risk Appetite Alignment

### Description
Decisions are made without reference to appetite.

### Indicators
- Risk acceptance without criteria
- Escalations ignored
- Appetite statements unused

### Impact
- Inconsistent decisions
- Leadership surprises

---

## 34.8 Failure 7: Poor Monitoring

### Description
Risks are assessed once and forgotten.

### Indicators
- No KRIs
- No trend analysis
- Infrequent reviews

### Impact
- Late detection
- Escalations after damage

---

## 34.9 Failure 8: Ineffective Escalation

### Description
Escalation paths exist but are unused.

### Indicators
- Fear of raising risks
- Unclear thresholds
- No leadership response

### Impact
- Small risks becoming major incidents

---

## 34.10 Failure 9: Siloed Risk Management

### Description
Risks are managed in isolation.

### Indicators
- No aggregation
- No cross-domain visibility
- Duplicate controls

### Impact
- Missed systemic risk
- Inefficient mitigation

---

## 34.11 Failure 10: Culture Misalignment

### Description
Culture discourages transparency.

### Indicators
- Blame-driven responses
- Punishing incident reporting
- Risk avoidance instead of management

### Impact
- Hidden risks
- Poor learning

---

## 34.12 Failure 11: Ignoring Emerging Risks

### Description
Focus remains on known risks only.

### Indicators
- No horizon scanning
- No innovation risk assessment
- No future scenarios

### Impact
- Strategic shocks
- Loss of competitiveness

---

## 34.13 Failure 12: Lack of Continuous Improvement

### Description
Lessons learned are not applied.

### Indicators
- Repeated incidents
- No post-mortems
- Static frameworks

### Impact
- Risk maturity stagnation

---

## 34.14 Preventing These Failures

Strong risk management requires:
- Leadership engagement
- Clear ownership
- Continuous monitoring
- Honest escalation
- Learning culture

Frameworks enable, people decide.

---

## 34.15 Summary

Risk management fails when:
- It becomes mechanical
- Judgment is absent
- Signals are ignored

Success depends on discipline and intent.
