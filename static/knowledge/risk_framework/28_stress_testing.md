# Chapter 28: Stress Testing

---

## 28.1 What Is Stress Testing

Stress testing is a structured method to:
- Evaluate resilience under extreme conditions
- Quantify impact beyond normal assumptions
- Test system and control breaking points

It asks:
“How bad can it get, and can we survive it?”

---

## 28.2 Stress Testing vs Scenario Analysis

| Aspect | Stress Testing | Scenario Analysis |
|------|---------------|------------------|
| Nature | Quantitative | Qualitative |
| Focus | Extreme conditions | Plausible futures |
| Output | Thresholds & limits | Narratives |
| Usage | Resilience testing | Preparedness |

Both are complementary.

---

## 28.3 Why Stress Testing Is Important

Stress testing helps organizations:
- Understand tail risks
- Identify hidden dependencies
- Validate risk tolerance
- Prepare for systemic shocks

Normal conditions hide fragility.

---

## 28.4 Types of Stress Tests

Common stress test types include:
- Sensitivity analysis
- Reverse stress testing
- Historical stress tests
- Hypothetical extreme scenarios

Different tests answer different questions.

---

## 28.5 Sensitivity Analysis

Sensitivity analysis examines:
- Impact of changes in one variable
- Control effectiveness under variation

It highlights key risk drivers.

---

## 28.6 Reverse Stress Testing

Reverse stress testing asks:
“What would have to happen to break us?”

It:
- Identifies failure thresholds
- Reveals unacceptable outcomes
- Challenges assumptions

This is powerful for governance.

---

## 28.7 Historical Stress Tests

These are based on:
- Past crises
- Known failures
- Industry incidents

History provides realism.

---

## 28.8 Hypothetical Extreme Scenarios

These test:
- Low-probability, high-impact events
- Combined failures
- Cascading impacts

Extreme does not mean unrealistic.

---

## 28.9 Defining Stress Parameters

Stress parameters may include:
- Financial loss levels
- Volume surges
- System outages
- Staff unavailability
- Regulatory actions

Parameters must be severe but credible.

---

## 28.10 Assessing Impact Under Stress

Stress testing evaluates:
- Financial viability
- Operational continuity
- Control breakdowns
- Recovery capability

Impact assessment must be multi-dimensional.

---

## 28.11 Stress Testing and Risk Appetite

Stress results should be compared against:
- Risk appetite
- Risk tolerance thresholds

If stress outcomes exceed tolerance, action is required.

---

## 28.12 Governance of Stress Testing

Governance ensures:
- Appropriate assumptions
- Senior review
- Documented outcomes
- Action tracking

Stress tests must influence decisions.

---

## 28.13 Frequency of Stress Testing

Stress testing should occur:
- Periodically
- After major changes
- After significant incidents
- When external risk increases

One-time stress tests are insufficient.

---

## 28.14 Common Failures in Stress Testing

Common failures include:
- Unrealistic assumptions
- Ignoring results
- Treating tests as compliance exercises
- No follow-up actions

Stress without action is pointless.

---

## 28.15 Using Stress Test Results

Results should inform:
- Capital and resource allocation
- Control enhancements
- Contingency planning
- Strategic decisions

Stress tests support resilience planning.

---

## 28.16 Summary

Stress testing:
- Reveals fragility
- Quantifies resilience
- Challenges assumptions
- Supports strategic risk decisions

It prepares organizations for the worst.
