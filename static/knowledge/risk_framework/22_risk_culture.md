# Chapter 22: Risk Culture

---

## 22.1 What Is Risk Culture

Risk culture is the set of **shared values, beliefs, and behaviors**
that influence how people perceive, discuss, and manage risk.

It determines:
- Whether risks are raised early
- Whether bad news is suppressed
- Whether controls are respected or bypassed

Culture operates when policies are silent.

---

## 22.2 Why Risk Culture Matters More Than Frameworks

Strong frameworks fail in weak cultures.

A poor risk culture leads to:
- Fear of escalation
- Blame avoidance
- Shortcut-taking
- Normalization of deviance

Culture decides whether rules are followed.

---

## 22.3 Indicators of a Healthy Risk Culture

Healthy risk cultures exhibit:
- Psychological safety
- Open discussion of issues
- Encouragement of escalation
- Accountability without blame
- Learning from failures

People speak up early.

---

## 22.4 Indicators of a Weak Risk Culture

Warning signs include:
- Fear of reporting issues
- Punishment for escalation
- Overconfidence
- “This is how we always do it”
- Risk discussions only after incidents

Silence is the loudest signal.

---

## 22.5 Leadership’s Role in Risk Culture

Leadership shapes culture through:
- What they reward
- What they tolerate
- What they ignore
- How they react to bad news

Tone from the top is decisive.

---

## 22.6 Risk Culture vs Compliance Culture

| Risk Culture | Compliance Culture |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Proactive | Reactive |
| Learning-focused | Rule-focused |
| Encourages escalation | Discourages deviation |
| Future-oriented | Past-oriented |

Compliance alone does not create safety.

---

## 22.7 Risk Culture and Incentives

Incentives strongly influence behavior.

Misaligned incentives cause:
- Risk concealment
- Metric gaming
- Control circumvention

Rewards must align with safe behavior.

---

## 22.8 Risk Culture Assessment

Risk culture can be assessed through:
- Surveys
- Incident analysis
- Escalation patterns
- Audit findings
- Behavioral observations

Culture leaves measurable traces.

---

## 22.9 Improving Risk Culture

Effective actions include:
- Leadership role modeling
- Clear escalation protection
- Learning reviews, not blame
- Training and communication
- Aligning incentives

Culture changes through consistency.

---

## 22.10 Risk Culture and Decision-Making

In healthy cultures:
- Decisions consider uncertainty
- Dissent is welcomed
- Risk trade-offs are explicit

In weak cultures:
- Speed overrides safety
- Questions are discouraged

---

## 22.11 Risk Culture in Practice

Strong risk culture means:
- Issues raised before harm occurs
- Controls respected even under pressure
- Learning from near misses

Culture is revealed under stress.

---

## 22.12 Summary

Risk culture:
- Determines real behavior
- Amplifies or undermines controls
- Requires leadership commitment
- Evolves over time

You cannot out-document a bad culture.
