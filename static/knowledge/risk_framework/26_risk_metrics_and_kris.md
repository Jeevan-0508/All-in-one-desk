# Chapter 26: Risk Metrics and Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)

---

## 26.1 Why Risk Metrics Matter

Risk that cannot be measured cannot be managed.

Risk metrics provide:
- Early warning signals
- Objective decision support
- Trend visibility
- Governance confidence

They shift organizations from reactive to proactive.

---

## 26.2 Risk Metrics vs Performance Metrics

| Metric Type | Focus |
|-----------|------|
| KPIs | Performance outcomes |
| KRIs | Risk exposure |
| Controls Metrics | Control effectiveness |

KPIs tell how well you are doing.  
KRIs tell how close you are to trouble.

---

## 26.3 What Are KRIs

Key Risk Indicators are measurable values that:
- Signal increasing risk exposure
- Correlate with potential incidents
- Trigger management attention

KRIs are predictive, not historical.

---

## 26.4 Characteristics of Effective KRIs

Good KRIs are:
- Relevant
- Measurable
- Timely
- Actionable
- Aligned to objectives

Too many KRIs reduce clarity.

---

## 26.5 Leading vs Lagging Indicators

| Type | Example |
|----|-------|
| Leading | Control failures, near misses |
| Lagging | Loss events, incidents |

Effective risk programs prioritize leading indicators.

---

## 26.6 Designing KRIs

KRI design starts with:
- Risk statements
- Risk drivers
- Control weaknesses

Each KRI must answer:
“What changes before this risk materializes?”

---

## 26.7 Thresholds and Limits

KRIs require thresholds:
- Green (acceptable)
- Amber (attention)
- Red (action)

Thresholds must align with risk appetite.

---

## 26.8 KRI Ownership

Each KRI must have:
- A clear owner
- Monitoring frequency
- Escalation rules

Unowned KRIs are ignored KRIs.

---

## 26.9 Data Sources for KRIs

Common sources include:
- Operational systems
- Audit findings
- Incident logs
- Third-party reports
- Manual assessments

Data quality is critical.

---

## 26.10 KRI Reporting

Effective KRI reporting is:
- Visual
- Concise
- Trend-based
- Decision-oriented

Dashboards must drive action, not decoration.

---

## 26.11 KRI Escalation

Escalation occurs when:
- Thresholds are breached
- Trends deteriorate
- Multiple KRIs correlate

KRIs lose value if escalation is ignored.

---

## 26.12 Common KRI Pitfalls

Common mistakes:
- Tracking too many indicators
- Using KPIs as KRIs
- No clear thresholds
- No response plan

Metrics without response create false comfort.

---

## 26.13 KRI Review and Refresh

KRIs must evolve as:
- Business changes
- Risks evolve
- Controls mature

Static KRIs become irrelevant.

---

## 26.14 KRIs and Governance

Governance bodies should:
- Review top KRIs regularly
- Challenge trends
- Demand corrective actions

KRIs enable informed oversight.

---

## 26.15 Examples of KRIs

Examples:
- % of overdue risk treatments
- Number of control exceptions
- Vendor SLA breaches
- Access violations
- Near-miss incidents

KRIs must reflect real exposure.

---

## 26.16 Summary

Risk metrics and KRIs:
- Provide early warning
- Enable proactive management
- Strengthen decision-making
- Support accountability

They are the nervous system of risk management.
