# Chapter 31: Risk in Programs and Projects

---

## 31.1 What Is Project and Program Risk

Project and program risk refers to uncertainty that may
affect objectives related to:
- Scope
- Schedule
- Cost
- Quality
- Benefits realization

Risk exists from initiation to closure.

---

## 31.2 Difference Between Project and Program Risk

| Project Risk | Program Risk |
|-------------|--------------|
| Single initiative | Multiple related projects |
| Tactical focus | Strategic focus |
| Shorter duration | Longer duration |
| Delivery-focused | Benefit-focused |

Programs amplify risk complexity.

---

## 31.3 Why Risk Management Is Critical in Delivery

Poor risk management leads to:
- Schedule overruns
- Budget overruns
- Scope creep
- Quality failures
- Stakeholder dissatisfaction

Most project failures are predictable.

---

## 31.4 Risk Integration into Project Lifecycle

Risk management must be embedded in:
1. Initiation
2. Planning
3. Execution
4. Monitoring & Control
5. Closure

It is not a standalone activity.

---

## 31.5 Risk Identification in Projects

Common sources include:
- Requirements ambiguity
- Resource constraints
- Dependencies
- Vendor performance
- Technology uncertainty
- Regulatory approvals

Early identification is key.

---

## 31.6 Risk Identification Techniques

Techniques include:
- Risk workshops
- Lessons learned reviews
- Dependency mapping
- Assumption analysis
- Expert judgment

Diverse perspectives improve coverage.

---

## 31.7 Risk Assessment in Projects

Assessment evaluates:
- Likelihood
- Impact (cost, schedule, scope, quality)
- Velocity (speed of impact)
- Detectability

Qualitative and quantitative methods are used.

---

## 31.8 Risk Prioritization

Risks are prioritized based on:
- Risk exposure
- Critical path impact
- Benefit impact
- Stakeholder sensitivity

Not all risks are equal.

---

## 31.9 Risk Response Strategies

Typical responses include:
- Avoid
- Mitigate
- Transfer
- Accept

Responses must be realistic and owned.

---

## 31.10 Risk Ownership

Each risk must have:
- A named owner
- Clear accountability
- Defined actions
- Escalation path

Unowned risks are unmanaged risks.

---

## 31.11 Risk Registers in Projects

Project risk registers track:
- Risk description
- Cause and impact
- Owner
- Status
- Mitigation actions

They must be living documents.

---

## 31.12 Risk in Program Management

Programs face additional risks such as:
- Inter-project dependencies
- Benefit realization risk
- Change fatigue
- Strategic misalignment

Program-level oversight is essential.

---

## 31.13 Dependency and Interface Risk

Risks often arise from:
- Cross-team dependencies
- External deliverables
- Shared resources
- Integration points

Mapping dependencies reduces surprises.

---

## 31.14 Change and Scope Risk

Changes introduce risk by:
- Increasing complexity
- Stretching timelines
- Diluting focus

Change control is a risk control.

---

## 31.15 Risk Escalation in Projects

Escalation occurs when:
- Impact exceeds tolerance
- Mitigation fails
- Deadlines are threatened

Escalation is a strength, not a failure.

---

## 31.16 Governance and Reporting

Effective governance ensures:
- Regular risk reviews
- Transparent reporting
- Leadership oversight
- Informed decisions

Visibility drives accountability.

---

## 31.17 Common Failures in Project Risk Management

Common failures include:
- Treating risk as documentation only
- Late identification
- Optimism bias
- Weak escalation

Discipline prevents repeat mistakes.

---

## 31.18 Risk and Agile / Hybrid Delivery

In Agile environments:
- Risks are reviewed iteratively
- Backlogs act as risk buffers
- Frequent feedback reduces uncertainty

Risk management adapts to delivery models.

---

## 31.19 Summary

Risk in programs and projects:
- Must be embedded, not isolated
- Requires continuous attention
- Protects delivery and benefits
- Enables informed trade-offs

Delivery excellence depends on risk discipline.
