# Chapter 05: Risk Governance

---

## 5.1 What Is Risk Governance

Risk governance defines **how risk-related authority, responsibility, and accountability are distributed** across the organization.

It answers:
- Who owns risk?
- Who approves risk decisions?
- Who monitors exposure?
- Who escalates issues?

Without governance, risk management becomes fragmented and ineffective.

---

## 5.2 Risk Governance vs Risk Management

| Aspect | Risk Governance | Risk Management |
|-----|-----------------|----------------|
| Focus | Direction & oversight | Execution |
| Ownership | Leadership | Business & risk teams |
| Decisions | Risk appetite, priorities | Identification, treatment |
| Time horizon | Strategic | Operational |

Governance sets the rules; management plays the game.

---

## 5.3 Key Components of Risk Governance

Effective risk governance includes:
- Clear ownership
- Defined roles
- Decision authority
- Escalation mechanisms
- Oversight and challenge

All components must work together.

---

## 5.4 The Three Lines of Responsibility (Conceptual Model)

### First Line: Business & Operations
- Own risks
- Execute controls
- Identify issues

### Second Line: Risk & Compliance Functions
- Define frameworks
- Provide guidance
- Challenge decisions
- Monitor effectiveness

### Third Line: Audit
- Independent assurance
- Validate governance and controls
- Report to leadership

This model prevents conflict of interest and blind spots.

---

## 5.5 Role of Leadership in Risk Governance

Leadership is responsible for:
- Setting risk appetite
- Approving risk tolerance
- Allocating resources
- Enforcing accountability

If leadership disengages, governance collapses.

---

## 5.6 Risk Appetite and Tolerance

### Risk Appetite
- The amount of risk the organization is willing to accept
- Set at leadership level

### Risk Tolerance
- Acceptable variation around objectives
- Applied at operational level

Clear appetite prevents:
- Over-control
- Reckless behavior
- Inconsistent decisions

---

## 5.7 Risk Ownership

Every risk must have:
- A single accountable owner
- Clear responsibility for mitigation
- Authority to act

Shared ownership often means **no ownership**.

---

## 5.8 Escalation and Decision Authority

Risk governance defines:
- When risks must be escalated
- Who can accept residual risk
- Who approves exceptions

Escalation should be:
- Timely
- Clear
- Non-punitive

Fear-based cultures hide risk.

---

## 5.9 Risk Committees and Forums

Common governance forums include:
- Risk committees
- Steering committees
- Executive reviews

These forums:
- Review high risks
- Approve treatment plans
- Track residual exposure

Meetings without decisions are governance failures.

---

## 5.10 Documentation and Traceability

Governance requires:
- Documented decisions
- Clear rationale
- Audit trails

Traceability enables:
- Accountability
- Regulatory compliance
- Learning

---

## 5.11 Common Governance Failures

Typical failures include:
- Undefined ownership
- Weak escalation paths
- Risk decisions made without authority
- Overreliance on risk teams

Governance failures amplify risk exposure.

---

## 5.12 Summary

Strong risk governance:
- Clarifies accountability
- Enables timely decisions
- Supports transparency
- Prevents unmanaged risk

Risk governance is a leadership responsibility.
