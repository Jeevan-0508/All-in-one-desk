# Chapter 30: Third-Party Risk

---

## 30.1 What Is Third-Party Risk

Third-party risk is the risk that arises when an organization
relies on external entities to deliver products or services.

These entities include:
- Vendors
- Suppliers
- Contractors
- Consultants
- Partners
- Outsourced service providers

The organization remains accountable.

---

## 30.2 Why Third-Party Risk Is Critical

Third parties can:
- Access sensitive data
- Affect operations
- Impact customers
- Introduce compliance exposure

Many major breaches originate from third parties.

---

## 30.3 Types of Third-Party Risks

Common categories include:
- Operational risk
- Cybersecurity risk
- Data privacy risk
- Compliance risk
- Financial risk
- Reputational risk
- Geopolitical risk

One vendor can introduce multiple risk types.

---

## 30.4 Third-Party Risk Lifecycle

The lifecycle includes:
1. Third-party identification
2. Risk classification
3. Due diligence
4. Risk assessment
5. Contractual controls
6. Ongoing monitoring
7. Termination and exit

Risk exists beyond onboarding.

---

## 30.5 Third-Party Identification

Organizations must identify:
- Direct third parties
- Subcontractors (fourth parties)
- Cloud service providers
- Logistics and payment partners

Visibility is the first control.

---

## 30.6 Risk Classification of Third Parties

Third parties are classified based on:
- Criticality
- Data access level
- Operational dependency
- Regulatory impact

Typical tiers:
- Low risk
- Medium risk
- High risk
- Critical risk

---

## 30.7 Due Diligence and Onboarding

Due diligence evaluates:
- Security posture
- Financial stability
- Compliance history
- Business continuity
- Legal exposure

Higher risk requires deeper review.

---

## 30.8 Third-Party Risk Assessment

Assessment includes:
- Inherent risk
- Control effectiveness
- Residual risk
- Risk acceptance decisions

Assessments must be documented.

---

## 30.9 Contractual Risk Controls

Contracts should include:
- Security requirements
- Data protection clauses
- Audit rights
- Incident notification timelines
- Termination rights

Contracts enforce accountability.

---

## 30.10 Ongoing Monitoring

Third-party risk must be monitored through:
- Periodic reassessments
- Performance metrics
- Incident tracking
- External intelligence

Risk changes over time.

---

## 30.11 Fourth-Party and Concentration Risk

Organizations must consider:
- Vendors relying on subcontractors
- Over-reliance on single suppliers
- Geographic concentration

Hidden dependencies amplify impact.

---

## 30.12 Third-Party Incidents

Third-party incidents may involve:
- Data breaches
- Service outages
- Compliance violations
- Fraud

Response must be immediate and coordinated.

---

## 30.13 Escalation and Reporting

High-risk issues should be:
- Escalated to leadership
- Reported to regulators if required
- Communicated internally

Delay increases damage.

---

## 30.14 Exit and Termination Risk

Risk does not end at contract closure.

Exit planning includes:
- Data return or destruction
- Access revocation
- Knowledge transfer
- Continuity planning

Poor exits create residual exposure.

---

## 30.15 Third-Party Risk Metrics

Common metrics include:
- Number of high-risk vendors
- Assessment completion rates
- Incident frequency
- SLA breaches
- Concentration levels

Metrics drive oversight.

---

## 30.16 Common Failures in Third-Party Risk

Frequent failures include:
- Treating onboarding as one-time
- Weak contracts
- No monitoring
- Ignoring fourth parties

Control gaps accumulate silently.

---

## 30.17 Summary

Third-party risk:
- Extends beyond organizational boundaries
- Requires lifecycle management
- Demands strong governance
- Is shared but not transferred

Accountability remains internal.
