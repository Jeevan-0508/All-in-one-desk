# Chapter 21: Risk Reporting

---

## 21.1 What Is Risk Reporting

Risk reporting is the structured communication of:
- Identified risks
- Risk status
- Trends and changes
- Decisions and actions

Its purpose is to support **informed decision-making**.

Reporting is not documentation — it is communication.

---

## 21.2 Why Risk Reporting Is Critical

Without effective reporting:
- Leadership is blind
- Risks escalate unnoticed
- Decisions are delayed
- Accountability weakens

Unreported risk is unmanaged risk.

---

## 21.3 Objectives of Risk Reporting

Risk reporting aims to:
- Provide visibility into risk exposure
- Highlight breaches of tolerance
- Track mitigation progress
- Enable escalation
- Support governance oversight

Reports exist to drive action.

---

## 21.4 Risk Reporting Audiences

Different audiences need different views:

- Board → Strategic risk exposure
- Executives → Aggregated trends
- Management → Operational detail
- Risk teams → Granular tracking

One report does not fit all.

---

## 21.5 Types of Risk Reports

Common report types include:
- Risk registers
- Risk dashboards
- Heat maps
- Trend analysis
- Incident summaries
- Executive briefings

Each serves a different purpose.

---

## 21.6 Risk Metrics and Indicators

Effective reporting uses:
- KRIs (Key Risk Indicators)
- Threshold breaches
- Control effectiveness metrics
- Incident frequency and severity

Metrics convert risk into signals.

---

## 21.7 Frequency of Risk Reporting

Reporting frequency depends on:
- Risk criticality
- Volatility
- Regulatory expectations

High-risk areas require more frequent reporting.

---

## 21.8 Escalation Through Reporting

Risk reports must:
- Clearly flag breaches
- Identify required decisions
- Specify owners and timelines

Reports without escalation paths are informational only.

---

## 21.9 Risk Reporting Quality Principles

Effective risk reports are:
- Accurate
- Timely
- Clear
- Action-oriented
- Honest

Optimistic reporting hides danger.

---

## 21.10 Risk Reporting and Governance

Risk reporting supports governance by:
- Informing committees
- Documenting decisions
- Enabling oversight
- Supporting accountability

Governance depends on reporting.

---

## 21.11 Common Failures in Risk Reporting

Common failures include:
- Overly complex reports
- Too much data, no insight
- Late reporting
- Suppressed bad news

Poor reporting creates false confidence.

---

## 21.12 Risk Reporting Maturity

Maturity levels include:
1. Manual, reactive reports
2. Standardized periodic reports
3. Dashboard-based reporting
4. Integrated enterprise reporting
5. Predictive and forward-looking insights

Maturity improves foresight.

---

## 21.13 Summary

Risk reporting:
- Makes risk visible
- Enables escalation
- Supports governance
- Drives decisions

If leadership cannot see risk, it cannot manage it.
