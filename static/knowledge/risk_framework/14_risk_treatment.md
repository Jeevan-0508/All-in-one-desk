# Chapter 14: Risk Treatment

---

## 14.1 What Is Risk Treatment

Risk treatment is the process of:
- Selecting and implementing actions
- To modify risk exposure
- In line with risk appetite and priorities

Risk treatment answers:
> “How will we handle this risk?”

Treatment is **deliberate action**, not reaction.

---

## 14.2 Risk Treatment vs Risk Acceptance

| Aspect | Risk Treatment | Risk Acceptance |
|-----|----------------|----------------|
| Action | Changes risk | Acknowledges risk |
| Objective | Reduce exposure | Tolerate exposure |
| Requirement | Controls / decisions | Approval & monitoring |

Every risk must be **treated or consciously accepted**.

---

## 14.3 Core Risk Treatment Strategies

There are four primary treatment options.

---

## 14.4 Risk Avoidance

Risk avoidance eliminates exposure by:
- Stopping the activity
- Changing the objective
- Redesigning the process

Examples:
- Not entering a high-risk market
- Decommissioning an unsafe system

Avoidance removes risk but may limit opportunity.

---

## 14.5 Risk Mitigation (Risk Reduction)

Mitigation reduces:
- Likelihood
- Impact
- Or both

Mitigation actions include:
- Implementing controls
- Improving processes
- Training staff
- Enhancing monitoring

This is the **most common treatment**.

---

## 14.6 Risk Transfer (Risk Sharing)

Risk transfer shifts exposure to another party.

Examples:
- Insurance
- Outsourcing
- Contractual indemnities

Important:
- Risk is transferred, not eliminated
- Residual risk always remains

---

## 14.7 Risk Acceptance

Risk acceptance means:
- Acknowledging residual risk
- Deciding no further action is required
- Monitoring the exposure

Acceptance requires:
- Appropriate authority approval
- Clear rationale
- Review timeline

Silent acceptance is a governance failure.

---

## 14.8 Selecting the Appropriate Treatment Option

Selection depends on:
- Risk severity
- Appetite and tolerance
- Cost-benefit analysis
- Feasibility
- Strategic impact

Not all risks should be mitigated aggressively.

---

## 14.9 Cost vs Risk Reduction Trade-Off

Risk treatment decisions must consider:
- Cost of controls
- Reduction in exposure
- Opportunity cost

Over-control can be as damaging as under-control.

---

## 14.10 Control Design in Risk Treatment

Controls may be:
- Preventive
- Detective
- Corrective

Effective treatment balances all three.

Poorly designed controls create false confidence.

---

## 14.11 Residual Risk After Treatment

After treatment:
- Risk rarely becomes zero
- Residual risk remains

Residual risk must:
- Be evaluated
- Be accepted or escalated
- Be monitored

---

## 14.12 Treatment Plans and Accountability

Treatment plans should include:
- Specific actions
- Owners
- Timelines
- Dependencies
- Success criteria

Unowned treatment plans fail.

---

## 14.13 Monitoring Treatment Effectiveness

Treatment effectiveness must be monitored using:
- KPIs
- Control testing
- Incident tracking
- Reviews

Ineffective treatment requires adjustment.

---

## 14.14 Common Risk Treatment Failures

Common failures include:
- Treating symptoms, not causes
- Over-reliance on manual controls
- Ignoring human behavior
- Failure to follow up

Treatment is a process, not a checkbox.

---

## 14.15 Summary

Risk treatment:
- Converts decisions into action
- Uses avoidance, mitigation, transfer, or acceptance
- Requires ownership and monitoring
- Balances risk reduction with cost and strategy

Good treatment reduces future incidents.
