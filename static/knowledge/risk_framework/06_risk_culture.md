# Chapter 06: Risk Culture

---

## 6.1 What Is Risk Culture

Risk culture refers to the **shared values, beliefs, and behaviors** that determine how people:
- Perceive risk
- Talk about risk
- Respond to risk
- Escalate issues
- Follow controls

Risk culture exists **whether formal frameworks exist or not**.

Frameworks describe *what should happen*.
Culture determines *what actually happens*.

---

## 6.2 Why Risk Culture Matters

Most major failures occur not because:
- Risks were unknown
- Controls did not exist

But because:
- Risks were ignored
- Issues were not escalated
- Bad news was suppressed
- Incentives rewarded the wrong behavior

Risk culture bridges the gap between **design and reality**.

---

## 6.3 Characteristics of a Strong Risk Culture

A strong risk culture includes:
- Psychological safety
- Open communication
- Accountability without blame
- Ethical decision-making
- Willingness to challenge assumptions

People feel safe to say:
> “This doesn’t feel right.”

---

## 6.4 Characteristics of a Weak Risk Culture

Warning signs of weak risk culture:
- Fear of escalation
- Blame-driven responses
- Suppression of bad news
- Overconfidence
- “Just get it done” mentality

Weak cultures hide risk until it becomes an incident.

---

## 6.5 Tone from the Top

Leadership behavior shapes culture more than policies.

Tone from the top is demonstrated by:
- How leaders react to bad news
- Whether escalation is rewarded or punished
- Whether controls are bypassed under pressure

If leaders ignore risk, others will too.

---

## 6.6 Incentives and Risk Behavior

People respond to incentives.

Misaligned incentives:
- Encourage shortcuts
- Discourage escalation
- Reward speed over safety

Aligned incentives:
- Reward transparency
- Encourage prevention
- Support long-term outcomes

Risk culture must align incentives with desired behavior.

---

## 6.7 Speak-Up Culture and Psychological Safety

Effective risk management requires:
- Freedom to raise concerns
- No retaliation for escalation
- Respect for dissenting views

Psychological safety enables:
- Early detection
- Honest reporting
- Continuous improvement

Silence is one of the biggest risks.

---

## 6.8 Risk Ownership and Empowerment

People must:
- Understand their risk responsibilities
- Have authority to act
- Be empowered to stop unsafe actions

Ownership without empowerment creates frustration.
Empowerment without accountability creates chaos.

---

## 6.9 Learning from Incidents

Strong risk cultures:
- Analyze failures objectively
- Focus on root causes
- Improve systems, not blame individuals

Weak cultures:
- Assign blame
- Move on quickly
- Repeat failures

Learning is a cultural capability.

---

## 6.10 Subcultures and Local Risk Behavior

Large organizations have:
- Multiple teams
- Different pressures
- Varying risk perceptions

Risk culture may differ across:
- Regions
- Functions
- Seniority levels

Frameworks must account for local realities.

---

## 6.11 Measuring Risk Culture

Risk culture can be assessed through:
- Surveys
- Incident trends
- Escalation patterns
- Audit findings
- Employee feedback

Culture is observable through behavior, not slogans.

---

## 6.12 Improving Risk Culture

Improving culture requires:
- Leadership commitment
- Consistent messaging
- Training and awareness
- Fair accountability
- Time and reinforcement

Culture cannot be fixed with a policy update.

---

## 6.13 Summary

Risk culture:
- Determines effectiveness of controls
- Influences escalation and transparency
- Shapes long-term resilience

Strong frameworks fail in weak cultures.
Strong cultures strengthen imperfect frameworks.
