# Chapter 20: Risk Governance

---

## 20.1 What Is Risk Governance

Risk governance is the **structure, processes, and oversight**
used to direct and control risk management across the organization.

It ensures:
- Risks are identified
- Decisions are made at the right level
- Accountability is enforced
- Oversight exists

Governance turns risk management into a system.

---

## 20.2 Why Risk Governance Is Critical

Without governance:
- Risks are managed inconsistently
- Escalations fail
- Decisions are undocumented
- Accountability is unclear

Strong governance prevents silent risk accumulation.

---

## 20.3 Risk Governance vs Risk Management

| Aspect | Risk Governance | Risk Management |
|-----|-----------------|-----------------|
| Focus | Oversight | Execution |
| Level | Strategic | Operational |
| Ownership | Leadership | Management |
| Purpose | Direction & control | Identification & mitigation |

Governance sets the rules.  
Management plays the game.

---

## 20.4 Key Components of Risk Governance

Effective risk governance includes:
- Clear roles and responsibilities
- Defined escalation paths
- Decision authorities
- Oversight committees
- Reporting mechanisms

Missing components weaken control.

---

## 20.5 Risk Committees and Oversight Bodies

Common governance bodies include:
- Board risk committee
- Executive risk committee
- Compliance committee
- Technology or cyber risk councils

Committees ensure cross-functional visibility.

---

## 20.6 Decision-Making Authority

Governance defines:
- Who can accept risk
- Who can approve residual risk
- Who can override controls
- Who must be informed

Authority must match risk impact.

---

## 20.7 Escalation Framework

Risk governance defines:
- Escalation triggers
- Escalation timelines
- Escalation recipients

Escalation is mandatory, not optional.

---

## 20.8 Risk Reporting and Transparency

Governance requires:
- Regular risk reporting
- Standardized formats
- Honest communication
- Visibility into trends

Poor reporting hides risk until failure occurs.

---

## 20.9 Risk Governance and Compliance

Governance ensures:
- Regulatory requirements are met
- Policies are enforced
- Controls are auditable
- Exceptions are approved

Compliance without governance is performative.

---

## 20.10 Risk Governance Maturity Levels

Governance maturity ranges from:
1. Ad-hoc
2. Reactive
3. Defined
4. Integrated
5. Optimized

Higher maturity reduces surprise risk.

---

## 20.11 Common Failures in Risk Governance

Common failures include:
- Committees without authority
- Decisions without documentation
- Escalations ignored
- Governance bypassed for speed

Weak governance amplifies risk.

---

## 20.12 Risk Governance in Practice

Effective governance means:
- Risks discussed openly
- Decisions documented
- Ownership enforced
- Oversight continuous

Governance is not bureaucracy — it is protection.

---

## 20.13 Summary

Risk governance:
- Provides oversight
- Defines authority
- Enables escalation
- Ensures accountability

Without governance, risk management collapses.
