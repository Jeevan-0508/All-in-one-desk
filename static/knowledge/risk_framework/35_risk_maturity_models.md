# Chapter 35: Risk Maturity Models

---

## 35.1 Purpose of Risk Maturity Models

Risk maturity models assess:
- How well risk is embedded
- How consistently risk is managed
- How proactively risks are addressed

They measure capability, not intent.

---

## 35.2 Why Risk Maturity Matters

Maturity determines:
- Predictability of outcomes
- Speed of response
- Quality of decisions
- Organizational resilience

Higher maturity = fewer surprises.

---

## 35.3 Overview of Maturity Levels

Most models define **five levels**:

1. Initial  
2. Repeatable  
3. Defined  
4. Managed  
5. Optimized  

Each level builds on the previous.

---

## 35.4 Level 1: Initial (Ad Hoc)

### Characteristics
- No formal framework
- Reactive risk handling
- Reliance on individuals

### Indicators
- Risks addressed after incidents
- No documentation
- No ownership

### Impact
- High exposure
- Frequent surprises

---

## 35.5 Level 2: Repeatable

### Characteristics
- Basic risk processes exist
- Some documentation
- Inconsistent application

### Indicators
- Risk registers exist
- Assessments vary by team
- Limited governance

### Impact
- Partial control
- Unpredictable effectiveness

---

## 35.6 Level 3: Defined

### Characteristics
- Organization-wide framework
- Defined roles and processes
- Standard assessment methods

### Indicators
- Consistent risk taxonomy
- Formal risk appetite
- Regular reporting

### Impact
- Improved visibility
- Better prioritization

---

## 35.7 Level 4: Managed

### Characteristics
- Risk integrated into decisions
- Data-driven monitoring
- Strong governance

### Indicators
- KRIs and KPIs tracked
- Escalation thresholds defined
- Leadership engagement

### Impact
- Early detection
- Controlled exposure

---

## 35.8 Level 5: Optimized

### Characteristics
- Proactive and predictive risk management
- Continuous improvement
- Risk-aware culture

### Indicators
- Scenario modeling
- Emerging risk programs
- Learning embedded in operations

### Impact
- Strategic advantage
- High resilience

---

## 35.9 Dimensions of Risk Maturity

Maturity is assessed across dimensions:
- Governance
- Culture
- Processes
- Data and technology
- Skills and capability

Weakest dimension limits overall maturity.

---

## 35.10 Assessing Risk Maturity

Assessment methods include:
- Self-assessments
- Independent reviews
- Benchmarking
- Audit findings

Honesty is critical.

---

## 35.11 Using Maturity Models

Organizations use maturity models to:
- Identify gaps
- Prioritize improvements
- Track progress over time
- Align investments

They guide evolution.

---

## 35.12 Common Maturity Pitfalls

Common issues include:
- Overestimating maturity
- Focusing only on documentation
- Ignoring culture
- Treating maturity as a checkbox

Maturity is behavioral.

---

## 35.13 Improving Risk Maturity

Improvement requires:
- Leadership commitment
- Clear ownership
- Training and awareness
- Technology enablement
- Continuous learning

Progress is incremental.

---

## 35.14 Maturity vs Compliance

Compliance ensures minimum standards.
Maturity ensures effectiveness.

Compliance is required.
Maturity is chosen.

---

## 35.15 Summary

Risk maturity models:
- Provide a roadmap
- Enable honest reflection
- Support long-term resilience

They turn frameworks into capability.
